# Part 0: Our company, Le Slip Français

*Case 6: Urbanization, Datamart / Datalake (multi-channel distribution)*

The brief describes a "retail company" selling through several channels, without naming it. To make our work concrete, we apply the case to a **real, well-known French company that is not a giant: Le Slip Français**. All facts below are public (the company has been listed on the stock exchange since July 2026). **The data we use in the proof of concept is synthetic**: generated by us to match these public figures, never the company's real data.

## 1. Who they are

| | |
|---|---|
| Activity | "Made in France" underwear and clothing: briefs, boxers, socks, pyjamas, T-shirts [1] |
| Founded | 2011 in Paris by Guillaume Gibault [1] |
| Revenue 2025 | **21.1 M€** (+16 %), the first profitable year: net result 0.7 M€ [2][3] |

| Stock market | Euronext Growth Paris since 14 July 2026 (ticker ALLSF), 13 M€ raised [5] |

## 2. Why it fits the case

The brief talks about a **multi-channel** retailer whose data is **siloed**. Le Slip Français sells through three channels:

| Channel | Share of revenue 2025 | What it looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| **Online** (own website) | **72 %** [3] (88 % in H1 2026 [4]) | Shopify Plus website since October 2023 [6] |
| **Own stores** | **9 %** [3] | The brand closed most of its ~20 stores in 2024–2025 [1]; its website lists **2 stores** today (Paris Marais and Toulouse) [8] |
| **Wholesale and others** | ~19 % (rest of revenue) | Permanent presence in about a hundred Carrefour hypermarkets since November 2024, and Monoprix [7] |

It is also a good example of the brief's challenge:

- **Channels moving fast:** from a store network to almost only online in two years, plus a new supermarket channel. Every change creates new reports and new silos.
- **A manufacturer and a retailer at the same time:** with its own factories, **inventory optimization** (one of the brief's three use cases) matters a lot: producing the right sizes and colours for the website, the stores and Carrefour.
- **A large customer base** (850,000 customers) with a strong CRM culture: this is exactly what **customer segmentation** needs.
- **Small team** (69 people): a heavy, complex platform would not suit them. This is important for our architecture choice.

## 4. The dataset: synthetic, sized on the public figures

No company publishes its real sales and customer data, and we must not use real personal data anyway (GDPR). So we will **generate a synthetic dataset** for all 7 sources, calibrated on the figures above.

To keep it **small**, we generate a **1 % sample** of the company's yearly activity, over 3 years (2023–2025):

| Source | Real yearly volume (estimated) | How we estimate it | Our dataset (1 %, per year) |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| E-commerce orders | ≈ 241,000 | 72 % × 21.1 M€ ÷ 63 € basket | ≈ 2,400 orders |
| Store tickets (POS) | ≈ 30,000 | 9 % × 21.1 M€ ÷ 63 € (same basket, assumed) | ≈ 300 tickets |
| Wholesale orders (ERP) | ≈ 5,200 | ~100 Carrefour hypermarkets × 1 order/week (assumed) | ≈ 52 orders |
| Customers | 850,000 (total) | public figure [3] | 8,500 customers |
| Products | ≈ 1,500 SKUs | about 300 products in several sizes/colours (assumed) | 1,500 SKUs (catalogue kept whole) |
| Stock (ERP) | 1 snapshot/week per SKU and location | 1,500 SKUs × 3 locations (warehouse + 2 stores) | ≈ 234,000 rows/year |
| CRM campaigns (Klaviyo) | ≈ 100 campaigns | about 2 campaigns per week (assumed) | 100 campaigns + sends to the sampled customers |
| Web analytics | ≈ 12 M sessions | 241,000 orders ÷ 2 % conversion rate (assumed) | 1 row per day per traffic source |
| Social media | daily metrics | 3 platforms | 1 row per day per platform |
| Support tickets | ≈ 12,000 | 5 % of orders (assumed) | ≈ 120 tickets |

Rules for the synthetic data:

- **Same formats as the real tools.** For example, Shopify orders as JSON-like records, Klaviyo events, and ERP tables. That way the integration problem of the brief (different formats) is real.
- **Realistic problems on purpose:** the same customer with different emails in Shopify and Klaviyo, store tickets without a customer, different refresh times. These are what the platform must fix.
- **Fake identities only.** No real names or emails: every person is generated.
- The generator will be written in the proof of concept (issue #8).

## 5. What this changes in Parts 1 to 4

Parts 1–4 were written for an imaginary retailer of **~30 stores, 60 M€ revenue and 70 report users**. Le Slip Français is smaller and more digital:

| Assumption | Before | Le Slip Français |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 60 M€ | 21.1 M€ |
| Stores | ~30 | 2 (+ ~100 Carrefour hypermarkets as wholesale) |
| Online share | small | 72–88 % |
| Employees | not stated | 69 |
| Power BI users | 70 (10 creators) | to be resized: at most about 25 (a third of the staff) |
| Inventory at stake (6.5 % of sales) | 3.9 M€/year | ≈ 1.4 M€/year |

These changes affect the costing (fewer licences and smaller volumes, but also a smaller inventory benefit), and they may affect the architecture choice. **Parts 1–4 should be updated with these figures** before we continue.
